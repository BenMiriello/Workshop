## 1. The thing that is still not done, and why it keeps looking that way

**Opening the Development app still lands on a chat list, and it still has a
Memory tab nobody asked for.** The operator has reported this in this chat and
in previous chats, more than once, and it is the single most repeated complaint
in the whole period.

What was actually fixed was the RENDERING of those tabs: readable rows instead
of a four-column grid that truncated every title to about twelve characters, a
search box, recency order, an app-name eyebrow so the page says which app it
belongs to. All real, all verified in screenshots.

**None of that was the complaint.** The complaint is that the app opens on a
generic chat list at all, and shows tabs it has no use for. That was never
touched, because it is a design question and every fix so far treated it as a
rendering question.

The mechanism: `frontend/src/components/shell/places.ts` gives EVERY app the
same eight rows (the app plus the seven in `APP_TABS`, in
`frontend/src/pages/apps/tabs/appTabState.ts`), and `useAppPlaces.ts` sets an
app's home to `/apps/<slug>`, which `AppHomeRedirect` in `App.tsx` sends to
`/apps/<slug>/chats`. So an app with no bundled UI opens on a chat list, and an
app with nothing in a tab still shows that tab.

`unlinkedReason` in `appTabState.ts` ALREADY computes whether a tab can have
content for a given app. It is currently used only to word an empty state. It
is the signal for whether to render the tab at all.

Filed as `7d8d733a`, with that mechanism written out. **This should be the next
piece of work.** It has been deferred three times.

### Background subagents work; the ban is deleted

The platform recorded for a year that background subagents "do not work in this
deployment" and enforced a PreToolUse hook denying them. The operator refused to
accept it. He was right.

Measured against the real CLI with this driver's own flags: Agent launched
background at 15.4s, `task_started`; the parent's `result` #1 at 18.8s; the
subagent's answer at 47.6s on the SAME process; `task_notification` at 47.7s;
the model answered and `result` #2 landed at 59.3s.

**The CLI was never the problem.** `_retire_turn_proc` SIGTERMs the whole
process group when `turn_end` says the turn is over, and the subagent is in that
group. It was killed 29 seconds before it would have spoken.

`turn_end.TurnProgress` now carries `background_pending` (`task_started` minus
`task_notification`, clamped) and a turn is not over while one is outstanding,
with its own long silence bound. `subagent_guard.py`, its test and the
`--settings` hook are deleted. 14 tests pin it. (`bbd7104c`)

Verified live in the same turn it shipped: a background subagent was launched,
work continued for about four minutes, and its result arrived in that reply.

## 3. Answering the tooling question

Asked: do chats and agents have, and know they have, a clock, a timer, a
reminder, a ping.

- **Clock.** Workshop injects none. `chat_context.py` has zero datetime
  references. The date arrives from the harness; there is no time of day and no
  clock tool. A build chat can run `date`; nothing said so until now.
- **Wake me later (relative).** Build chats have Bash `run_in_background` (one
  completion callback) and Monitor (one event per occurrence). Narrow chats have
  neither. Now stated accurately per chat.
- **Wake me at a time (absolute).** `CronCreate` exists but its jobs die when
  the turn's process is retired. **Nothing an agent can call from inside a chat
  arms a durable wakeup.** The Workshop scheduler is real and durable but is
  configured through HTTP routes for the operator, not exposed as a tool. This
  is a genuine capability gap, not just an advertising gap.
- **Ping.** `WORKSHOP_NOTIFY_FILE` is real and plumbed; its writers are Jobs and
  scripts. A build chat could append to it via Bash. No tool wraps it and
  nothing advertises it.

The durable-wakeup gap is the one worth building. It is not filed yet.

## 6. Two process lessons, both paid for

**A uniform metric hides the defect.** A 39-route sweep measuring console
errors, failed requests and rendered text length reported everything clean while
the Tickets page showed nothing over 188 tickets and the Memory tab scored one
of the HIGHEST content scores on the board while being a wall of truncated
titles. The proxy did not merely miss the defect, it ranked it well. A uniform
check establishes a floor ("nothing crashed"), never a ceiling ("everything
works"), and reporting the floor in the ceiling's voice is a false all-clear.
The check that found every real bug was comparing the source of truth to what is
rendered, which is per-page and does not generalise into a script.

**A recorded conclusion is not evidence.** "We could not make background
subagents work" was written down as "they do not work here", and that survived a
year and a hook because nobody re-ran the experiment against the part of the
system we control. When a design doc explains why something is impossible, check
whether the impossibility is ours.

## 7. The regression pattern, stated plainly

The operator's read at the end of this period was that the work has been going
backwards. That is fair, and the record supports it. In three days:

- The dock was made to render every database row, which drew tiles reading
  `D(` and `GE`. Reverted the next turn.
- The app Chats tab was "fixed" in a way that listed 59 conversations that 404
  when opened. That was a decision made in a comment ("a row the fold does not
  have still comes back, thin") and never checked against the data.
- A guard added to stop junk directories refused every relative path, which
  broke `project_tasks.py <verb> .`, the documented interface named in the agent
  instructions themselves.
- Wiring pytest into the reviewer made the suite recurse and stop finishing,
  which is precisely the failure the gate was added to prevent.

Every one of those was found and fixed within a turn or two, but that is not the
point: each shipped because the change was verified against the thing it was
supposed to fix and nothing else. The pattern is **narrow verification**. A fix
is checked by the check that motivated it, not by using the system the way the
operator uses it.

Two habits follow, and they are cheap:

1. **After changing a shared mechanism, run the thing that consumes it.** The
   store guard needed one `project_tasks.py` invocation. The gate needed one
   full suite run. Both were one command.
2. **Fix the complaint, not the nearest defect to the complaint.** The
   Development app landing is the standing example: three rounds of work went
   into how those tabs render because rendering was the visible defect, while
   the actual complaint (it opens on a chat list and has tabs nobody asked for)
   went untouched and unfiled until section 1 of this document.

The second is the expensive one. It is why the same report keeps coming back.
