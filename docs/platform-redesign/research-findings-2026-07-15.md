# External research findings for the coherent rebuild (2026-07-15)

Scope: web research on the six mechanics of the Project/Agent/Chat/Task/Run model: ralph loops + reviewer gates, background orchestration + self-healing, embedding dedup, ambient context + cache-stable prefixes, quota governance, and LLM-as-judge reliability. Claims are cited; where a point is my own inference from the sources rather than a stated claim, it is marked "(inference)".

## Executive summary

- **LLMs cannot reliably self-verify.** Intrinsic self-correction without external signals fails and often makes answers worse (ICLR'24, Huang et al.). The ralph reviewer gate is only trustworthy if it is grounded in executable evidence (tests, commands, artifacts), not in reading the worker's prose or even just the diff.
- **Verification failure is a first-class, measured failure category.** The MAST taxonomy attributes 21.3% of multi-agent failures to verification/termination problems, and found prompt-only fixes buy just 5-14%; structural verifiers (which our fresh-context reviewer is) are the recommended fix. Our design is directionally right here.
- **Thrash is the dominant ralph failure mode in the wild.** Community ralph implementations converge on: hard iteration caps (start ~10), repeated-identical-action detection (warn at 4-6 repeats, halt at 5+), and dual-condition exit gates. We currently have none of these inside a single run; parking only happens at sweep level.
- **The ambient digest as designed likely has a cache bug and a context-rot bug.** A changing digest inside the "cache-stable prefix" invalidates everything after it on every change (Anthropic caching docs, Manus). And Chroma's context-rot study shows topically-related distractors (which a list of open tasks literally is) measurably degrade performance even far below the context limit. The digest must sit after the cache breakpoint, and be small and ranked.
- **Cosine thresholds are model-relative and axis-blind.** 0.85-0.9 is the standard near-duplicate band, so our 0.90 merge is in range, but published pitfalls show high-cosine pairs that differ on one critical axis get falsely merged. Merges should be non-destructive and the 0.80-0.90 gray zone should get a cheap LLM pairwise check.
- **Fencing only works if the store checks the token.** Kleppmann: the fence token must be monotonic and rejected-if-stale by the resource at write time; a token the worker merely holds is not a fence. Lease TTLs must be sized for multi-minute LLM latency with in-loop heartbeats at < 50% of the timeout (Temporal guidance).
- **Token spend predicts agent performance, and multi-agent costs ~15x chat** (Anthropic). Budget-aware effort scaling belongs in the task description itself ("simple task: 1 worker, few tool calls"), not only in the admission ledger.
- **For the reviewer gate, pairwise-judge biases mostly don't apply, but leniency drift and worker gaming do.** The judge must see artifacts and command output, never the worker's self-report; verdicts should be a fixed rubric checklist with structured reasons; sample verdicts should be operator-spot-checked.

## 1. Autonomous coding agent loops (the ralph loop + reviewer gate)

**What the sources say.**

- The ralph pattern's whole mechanism is *fresh context per iteration*, with the filesystem (code, TODO, git) as memory instead of conversation history: "fresh context every iteration. That's not a side effect, it's the point" ([Thomas Wiegold](https://thomas-wiegold.com/blog/ralph-loop-how-recursive-ai-agents-work/), [Geoffrey Huntley, "everything is a ralph loop"](https://ghuntley.com/loop/)). Huntley's operational stance: when you see a failure domain, fix the harness so it never recurs; keep the loop monolithic and simple (~300 lines) rather than jumping to distributed multi-agent complexity.
- Community hardening of ralph converged on the same guardrails independently: [ralph-claude-code](https://github.com/frankbria/ralph-claude-code) adds "intelligent exit detection, dual-condition exit gates, rate limiting, and circuit breakers"; [paddo.dev's writeup](https://paddo.dev/blog/ralph-wiggum-autonomous-loops/) documents that string-match completion promises are unreliable, that "Claude self-assesses completion, which... is problematic," recommends starting with a 10-iteration hard cap, prefers programmatic validation (tests passing, builds succeeding) over self-assessment, and lists "loops that burn through iterations without converging" as the primary observed failure mode. Also: "a numerical cap does not prevent an agent from deleting a database in the second iteration," i.e. caps bound cost, not damage.
- **Self-correction research**: [Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet (ICLR'24)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) shows intrinsic self-correction (no external feedback) barely fixes anything (GPT-3.5 fixed 7.6% of wrong answers while breaking 8.8% of right ones) and that the fix is high-quality *external* feedback: tools, tests, execution. This is the strongest single argument for what makes reviewer-gated loops converge vs. thrash: the gate needs ground truth, not another opinion.
- **Doom-loop detection** is now a documented pattern: SWE-bench harnesses inject warnings when a command repeats 4-6 times; SPOQ halts with human escalation at 5+ identical commands ([SPOQ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03115), [hermes-agent issue on Kilocode-style detection](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/512), [Unblocked on doom loops](https://getunblocked.com/blog/ai-agent-doom-loop/)). [Verdent's guide](https://www.verdent.ai/guides/tutorial/build-coding-agent-loop) states the rule plainly: decide upfront iteration caps, token/time ceilings, and escalation triggers, because a loop that can't solve the problem "doesn't fail, it spins."
- A related observation on stuck sessions: "adding more context to a stuck session adds more weight behind the wrong approach" ([When Your Coding Agent Says Done (And Isn't)](https://aiweekender.substack.com/p/when-your-coding-agent-says-done)). Feeding the full failed history back into a retry entrenches the failure; fresh context on retry is correct.

**Concrete recommendations for this system.**

1. **Ground the ralph reviewer gate in execution.** Require every Task's acceptance criteria to include at least one machine-checkable assertion (command + expected outcome) where feasible; the reviewer runs them and its verdict must cite their output. LLM-only judgment is the fallback, not the default. (Direct application of ICLR'24 + paddo.dev.)
2. **Add in-run no-progress detection.** Park the task (dead-letter) when: the reviewer rejects with substantially the same reason twice, OR two consecutive iterations produce near-identical diffs, OR the same tool call repeats ~5 times. Today parking is only sweep-level; the thrash happens *inside* a run. (Thresholds from SWE-bench/SPOQ practice.)
3. **Dual-condition exit + hard caps per run:** completion requires reviewer approval AND acceptance checks green; every run gets a hard iteration cap (default ~10) and a token ceiling that feeds the usage ledger.
4. **On rejection, restart the worker with fresh context** containing only: the task, the rejection reasons, and the current repo state. Do not carry the failed attempt's transcript forward. (Inference from the ralph fresh-context principle + the stuck-session observation; if the worker is already fresh per iteration, keep it that way and resist "helpfully" appending history.)
5. Already sound: the fresh-context reviewer as a *separate* agent is exactly the structural verifier MAST recommends over prompt tweaks (below).

## 2. Multi-agent orchestration, task queues, self-healing

**What the sources say.**

- **MAST taxonomy** ([Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v1)): 14 failure modes across 150+ tasks and 5 frameworks; specification/design failures 41.8%, inter-agent misalignment 36.9%, verification/termination 21.3%. Notable modes for us: FM-1.3 step repetition, FM-1.5 unaware of termination conditions, FM-3.1 premature termination, FM-3.2/3.3 no/incorrect verification. Prompt-level mitigations yielded only ~5% (AG2) to ~14% (ChatDev) gains; the authors call for structural redesigns (dedicated verifier roles, clearer topologies).
- **Anthropic's multi-agent research system** ([How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)): vague subagent tasking caused duplicated/overlapping work; the fix was task descriptions with explicit objective, output format, tool guidance, and boundaries, plus effort-scaling rules embedded in prompts ("simple fact-finding requires just 1 agent with 3-10 tool calls... complex research might use more than 10 subagents"). Token usage explained ~80% of performance variance; multi-agent burns ~15x chat tokens. Production practices: rainbow deployments, resumability from durable state, and letting the orchestrator, not the worker, decide when to stop.
- **Fencing tokens** ([Kleppmann, How to do distributed locking](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2016/02/08/how-to-do-distributed-locking.html)): a lease alone is unsafe under GC pauses/slow workers; the fix is a *monotonically increasing* token that the storage layer actively checks, "rejecting any writes on which the token has gone backwards." A random claim UUID that only the worker knows is not a fence unless every state-mutating write is validated against it. Follow-up caveat: fencing does not cover the case where a lower-token write arrives after a higher-token write at a resource that can't order them ([Surfing Complexity, Locks, leases, fencing tokens, FizzBee!](https://surfingcomplexity.blog/2025/03/03/locks-leases-fencing-tokens-fizzbee/)); for a single-writer JSON file with compare-and-swap this is manageable, but only if the CAS is real.
- **Queues/DLQ canon**: at-least-once delivery is the standard; make consumers idempotent (unique keys + unique constraints / SET NX) rather than chasing exactly-once ([task-queues.com](https://www.task-queues.com/queue-fundamentals-architecture/exactly-once-vs-at-least-once-delivery/)); route to DLQ after maxReceiveCount instead of retrying forever, since "a single poison message can saturate a partition"; monitor DLQ ingestion velocity as an early systemic-corruption signal ([AWS SQS DLQ docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSSimpleQueueService/latest/SQSDeveloperGuide/sqs-dead-letter-queues.html), [ctaverna, Design for failure with DLQs](https://ctaverna.github.io/dead-letters/)); retries use exponential backoff with jitter ([Background Jobs and Queues 2026 reference](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/background-job-queue-patterns-2026-engineering-reference)).
- **Lease sizing for LLM workers** ([xgrid, Temporal AI Agent Failures](https://www.xgrid.co/resources/temporal-ai-agent-orchestration-failure-patterns/)): LLM inference legitimately takes minutes, so short default leases cause premature reclaim storms; set timeouts calibrated to inference latency with headroom, heartbeat at < 50% of the timeout, and emit heartbeats *from inside the work loop*, not just at start. Uncapped concurrency causes cascading timeouts; cap worker concurrency to actual capacity.

**Concrete recommendations for this system.**

1. **Make claim_token a real fence.** Every mutation of a task in `.tasks.json` (status change, completion, notes) must be a compare-and-swap that rejects if the stored claim_token differs or if the mutation's fence generation is older. Prefer a per-task monotonic counter incremented on each (re)claim over a bare UUID, so a resurrected zombie worker's late write is *ordered out*, not just mismatched. (Kleppmann.)
2. **Size leases for LLM latency and renew via heartbeat.** Workers renew the lease from inside the ralph iteration loop at < 50% of lease TTL; reclaim only fires on missed heartbeats, not a fixed wall-clock guess. (Temporal guidance.)
3. **Idempotency by task effect, not just claim.** A reclaimed task will re-run; require workers to check current repo/world state against acceptance criteria before redoing work (the ralph "filesystem is memory" property gives this nearly for free, but state it as a contract). (Queue canon; inference for the ralph specialization.)
4. **Park counts + park velocity metric.** Park after N failed *runs* (not sweeps) per task; alert/surface when parking velocity spikes, since that indicates a systemic problem (bad prompt template, broken tool), not N bad tasks. (SQS practice.)
5. **Circuit breaker: add a half-open probe.** After cool-off, admit exactly one probe task; only close the breaker on its success. Full reopening after a timer just re-triggers the failure storm. (Standard breaker pattern; inference applied to sweep scopes.)
6. **Ticket schema: adopt Anthropic's subagent fields.** The chat-to-Task distiller should emit: objective, output format, suggested tools/entry points, explicit boundaries ("do not touch X"), and an effort class that maps to iteration/token caps. This attacks MAST's top category (specification failures, 41.8%) at the source.

## 3. Task deduplication with embeddings

**What the sources say.**

- Common operating points: ~0.85 cosine is "a widely recognized optimal high-precision cutoff for near-duplicate content"; ~0.9 is used as a strict duplicate threshold; in domains with homogeneous vocabulary, thresholds must be tuned upward because everything looks similar ([CodeSignal dedup lesson](https://codesignal.com/learn/courses/optimized-data-preparation-for-large-scale-llms/lessons/dataset-deduplication-and-redundancy-removal), [distilabel EmbeddingDedup docs](https://argilla-io.github.io/distilabel/dev/components-gallery/steps/embeddingdedup/), [Foundation-Sec-8B report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01059)).
- Key published pitfall: even at 0.9+, lexically similar texts that differ on one critical axis get spuriously merged; a clinical-NLP paper had to add explicit exclusion rules on top of cosine to prevent merging distinct statements ([Modeling Clinical Uncertainty in Radiology Reports](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04506)). Tasks have exactly this shape: "fix auth on endpoint A" vs "fix auth on endpoint B" can be near-identical in embedding space while being two units of work.
- Thresholds are embedding-model-relative: the meaningful cosine range differs by model (Chroma's context-rot work casually spans 0.445-0.829 across embedding models for "similar" pairs), so a threshold calibrated for one model silently drifts if the model changes. (Threshold-drift framing is my inference; the model-relativity is sourced.)

**Concrete recommendations for this system.**

1. **Keep 0.90 as the merge trigger but make merging two-stage and non-destructive.** At >= 0.90, do not auto-merge on cosine alone: run a cheap LLM pairwise check ("same unit of work? Y/N + reason") and on merge, supersede-with-link (keep the loser's provenance, point it at the winner) rather than deleting. False merges silently drop work, which is the worst failure of the whole ticket pipeline.
2. **Constrain dedup scope.** Only compare within the same scope/project queue. Cross-project cosine hits should at most create "related" links, never merges. (Inference from the critical-axis pitfall.)
3. **Canonicalization**: strip provenance, timestamps, chat-specific phrasing before hashing/embedding, but *keep concrete identifiers* (file paths, endpoint names) in the embedded text, since those are exactly the critical axes that distinguish near-identical tasks. (Inference.)
4. **Pin the embedding model + threshold together.** Record the embedding model name in `.tasks.json` entries; on model change, invalidate stored vectors and re-embed rather than comparing across models. (Sourced model-relativity, applied.)
5. The 0.80-0.90 "link related" band is well within community practice and is the right non-destructive default for the gray zone; sound as designed.

## 4. Context and memory: ambient digest, cache-stable prefixes, learnings

**What the sources say.**

- **Context rot** ([Chroma, Context Rot](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)): across 18 frontier models, performance degrades non-uniformly as input grows, even on trivial tasks and even far below the window limit (serious loss can appear at 50K in a 200K window per [Morph's summary](https://www.morphllm.com/context-rot)). Two findings hit our digest directly: (a) *even a single topically-related distractor reduces accuracy*, and distractor impact amplifies with length; (b) lower needle-question similarity accelerates degradation. A digest of open tasks and in-flight jobs is, for any given turn, a list of topically-related distractors.
- **Anthropic context engineering** ([Effective context engineering for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)): guiding principle is "the smallest set of high-signal tokens"; techniques are compaction, structured note-taking (persist notes outside the window, pull back later), and sub-agents. Our learnings store is a form of structured note-taking, which this source validates.
- **Cache-stable prefixes** ([Manus, Context Engineering lessons](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus), [Anthropic prompt caching docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)): a single-token change invalidates the KV cache from that token onward; keep the prefix stable (no timestamps), append-only, deterministic serialization; cache reads are ~10x cheaper than uncached input ($0.30 vs $3/MTok on Sonnet-class models). Anthropic docs are explicit: put the cache breakpoint on the last block whose prefix is identical across requests, and put varying content *after* it; changes cascade (tools -> system -> messages).
- **Learnings distillation lineage**: [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366) (store verbal reflections on failures, condition next attempt on them), ExpeL (aggregate recurring patterns across trajectories into reusable insights), Voyager (lifelong skill library grown from successful programs), surveyed in [Memory in LLMs: Mechanisms, Evaluation and Evolution](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.18868). Our rejected-reviewer-reasons-to-learnings pipeline is Reflexion-shaped; skills-from-repeated-patterns is Voyager/ExpeL-shaped. Both are validated patterns.

**Concrete recommendations for this system.**

1. **Fix the digest/cache contradiction.** "Ambient digest inside the cache-stable prefix" is self-contradicting: every task state change would invalidate the cached prefix for every chat. Place the digest immediately *after* the cache breakpoint (start of the non-cached suffix), or pin a digest epoch per chat session and only roll it at turn boundaries when it materially changed. Given the 10x read discount, this is likely the single largest cost lever in the design.
2. **Cap and rank the digest.** Do not inject all open tasks + jobs. Inject top-K (K small, ~5-7) ranked by relevance to the chat's scope, one line each, with a count of the remainder ("+9 more; ask to list"). Chroma's distractor finding says every irrelevant entry actively costs accuracy, not just tokens.
3. **Make the digest queryable instead of fully ambient.** Ambient gets the headline ("3 tasks in flight in this project"); detail comes via a tool call when the model needs it. This matches Anthropic's smallest-high-signal-set principle. (Inference from sourced principles.)
4. **Learnings hygiene budget.** The learnings store needs the same dedup + cap discipline as tasks: fixed token budget per scope in the prompt, age-out or demote learnings that stop being retrieved, and merge near-duplicates; an ever-growing guardrail list is context rot self-inflicted. (Inference from Chroma + Anthropic principle; mechanism validated by Reflexion/ExpeL.)
5. **No timestamps or counters in the stable prefix** (Manus). Audit the prefix builder for anything that changes per request.

## 5. Cost and quota governance

**What the sources say.**

- Agentic systems have "no natural spending ceiling": retry storms, loops, and sub-agent spawning burn budget without any single authorizing decision ([Finout, Agentic AI Cost Governance](https://www.finout.io/blog/agentic-ai-cost-governance-controlling-spend-before-it-controls-you)). Industry practice is layered: per-request ceilings, per-session rolling budgets, per-key caps, model-tier routing, and cost circuit breakers ([AI Security Gateway, 5-layer token budgets](https://aisecuritygateway.ai/blog/llm-token-budget-strategies-for-agents)); tag every call with agent/workflow identity for attribution ([iSimplifyMe, AI Agent Cost Governance](https://isimplifyme.com/blog/ai-agent-cost-governance)); Microsoft ships a governance toolkit tutorial on cost/token budgets ([agent-governance-toolkit](https://github.com/microsoft/agent-governance-toolkit/blob/main/docs/tutorials/24-cost-and-token-budgets.md)). An empirical catalog of 63 budget-overrun incidents exists ([Token Budgets, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04056)).
- **Weighted max-min / DRF**: weighted max-min fairness is the canonical single-resource allocation; DRF ([Ghodsi et al., NSDI'11](https://amplab.cs.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/Dominant-Resource-Fairness-Fair-Allocation-of-Multiple-Resource-Types.pdf), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_resource_fairness)) generalizes it to multiple resources with strategy-proofness, envy-freeness, Pareto efficiency. With one resource (tokens in a 5h window), plain weighted max-min is the right tool; DRF only becomes relevant if we later meter multiple resources (tokens + wall-clock worker slots, say).
- Anthropic's finding that token usage explains ~80% of performance variance, and that multi-agent costs ~15x chat, means effort classing is a quality decision, not only a cost decision ([Anthropic multi-agent blog](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

**Concrete recommendations for this system.**

1. **Two-point enforcement, not just admission.** The rolling 5h ledger admits work; add a mid-run kill: each Run carries a token allowance (from its effort class), workers report usage per iteration, and the dispatcher cancels runs that exceed allowance instead of discovering it at the next sweep. Admission-only control cannot stop a runaway run. (Layered-budget practice.)
2. **Reserve interactive headroom.** Autonomous work should only be admitted up to a fraction (say 60-70%) of the 5h budget, leaving guaranteed headroom for the operator's interactive chats; autonomous work always yields. (Inference; consistent with per-session budget layering.)
3. **Attribution tags on every call**: project, task id, run id, role (worker/reviewer/distiller). Required for the weighted max-min weights to mean anything and for postmortems on overruns. (Sourced practice.)
4. The weighted max-min allocation itself is sound and canonical for a single metered resource; don't upgrade to DRF unless a second scarce resource is actually metered.
5. **Count reviewer + distiller tokens against the same scope budget** as the worker. The 15x multiplier in multi-agent setups comes precisely from the auxiliary calls nobody meters. (Inference from Anthropic's token economics.)

## 6. LLM-as-judge / reviewer reliability

**What the sources say.**

- Documented biases: position bias, verbosity/length bias, self-preference (a model rates its own outputs ~10% higher win rate: [Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819)), concreteness bias; minor prompt changes destabilize verdicts ([A Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15594v6), [Judging the Judges: position bias study, 150k+ instances](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)).
- Mitigations with support: decompose criteria into granular rubric items rather than holistic scores; structured output with reasons; position swapping / majority voting across instances; pairwise beats absolute scoring for human alignment; reference-guided judging with anchor examples ([survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15594v6)). Anthropic used a single LLM judge with an explicit rubric plus human spot-checks and found that more reliable than multi-judge complexity at their scale ([multi-agent blog](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).
- For *completion gating* specifically (binary pass/fail vs acceptance criteria), position/verbosity biases matter less than: leniency drift, the worker gaming the judge with persuasive self-reports, and self-preference when judge and worker are the same model family. MAST found "no or incomplete verification" and "incorrect verification" are distinct, both common; a verifier that exists but checks the wrong thing still fails (FM-3.3) ([MAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v1)).

**Concrete recommendations for this system.**

1. **The reviewer judges artifacts, never testimony.** Input = task + acceptance criteria + diff + outputs of executed acceptance checks. Explicitly exclude the worker's summary/self-report from the reviewer's context (it is persuasion, and self-preference bias means same-family models are receptive to it). Our "fresh-context reviewer reviews the diff" design is right; make the exclusion of worker prose an enforced property of the pipeline, not a convention.
2. **Rubric checklist, per-criterion verdicts.** The reviewer returns `{criterion, pass, evidence}` per acceptance criterion plus an overall gate; holistic "looks done" verdicts are the unreliable mode. (Survey: decomposed criteria + structured output.)
3. **Determinism hygiene**: fixed reviewer prompt template, temperature 0-ish, and version the template so verdict drift can be traced to template changes. (Survey: prompt instability.)
4. **Escalation, not re-roll.** Do not re-run the reviewer hoping for a pass (that converts the gate into a slot machine); a rejected run iterates the worker, and repeated rejection parks the task for the operator. (Inference; consistent with majority-vote guidance being about *bias reduction*, not verdict shopping.)
5. **Operator spot-check sample.** Periodically surface a small random sample of approved completions for human review; this is Anthropic's practice and the only defense against slow leniency drift. Cheap: one Discord digest a week. (Sourced practice, sized for a single operator.)

## Prioritized changes

Ranked. Effort: S < M < L.

1. **Move the ambient digest out of the cached prefix** (place after the cache breakpoint, or epoch-pin per session). The current wording implies every task change invalidates every chat's cache; at 10x read discount this is the biggest cost bug in the design. (S)
2. **Ground the ralph reviewer in executed acceptance checks**, with per-criterion `{pass, evidence}` output and worker self-reports excluded from its context. Self-correction research says an ungrounded LLM gate will approve wrong work. (M)
3. **In-run no-progress detection**: park on repeated rejection reason, near-identical consecutive diffs, or ~5 identical tool calls; plus a hard iteration cap (~10) and per-run token allowance with mid-run kill. This is where thrash actually happens; sweep-level breakers won't catch it. (S-M)
4. **Cap and rank the ambient digest** (top ~5-7 scoped entries, one line each, remainder as a count + tool call for detail). Chroma: every related-but-irrelevant entry measurably degrades the model. (S)
5. **Make claim_token a checked, monotonic fence**: per-task generation counter, CAS on every `.tasks.json` mutation, stale generations rejected. A held-but-unchecked UUID does not prevent zombie-writer corruption. (M)
6. **Lease renewal via in-loop heartbeat** at < 50% of TTL, TTL sized for multi-minute LLM latency; reclaim on missed heartbeats only. Fixed short leases will cause spurious reclaims of healthy slow workers, the classic Temporal-with-LLMs failure. (S-M)
7. **Two-stage, non-destructive dedup**: at >= 0.90 add a cheap LLM same-work check before merging; merges supersede-with-link; dedup scope limited to the same queue; embedding model pinned and recorded. False merges silently delete work. (S-M)
8. **Ticket schema upgrade**: objective, output format, boundaries, entry points, effort class (mapped to caps) in every distilled Task. Attacks the largest MAST failure category (specification, 41.8%) at creation time. (S)
9. **Learnings store hygiene**: fixed prompt token budget per scope, dedupe, age-out unretrieved entries. Otherwise the anti-context-rot mechanism becomes a context-rot source. (M)
10. **Budget: reserve interactive headroom + meter reviewer/distiller calls** against the same scope budgets, with attribution tags on every call. (M)
11. **Circuit breaker half-open probe**: one probe task after cool-off, close only on success. (S)
12. **Weekly operator spot-check digest** of a random sample of reviewer-approved completions, to catch leniency drift. (S)

**Where the design is already sound** (per the sources, not just self-congratulation): fresh-context reviewer as a separate structural verifier (MAST's recommended fix); filesystem-as-memory ralph workers (the canonical pattern); dead-letter parking + circuit breaker (queue canon, just needs the refinements above); the 0.90/0.80 two-tier dedup bands (within published practice); rejected-reasons-as-learnings (Reflexion-shaped, validated); weighted max-min over a single token budget (canonical, no need for DRF yet).
